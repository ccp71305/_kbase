# Speed Reading & Sub-vocalization Reduction Plan

A 6-week, trackable program. Check boxes as you complete sessions. Log numbers in the tracking table at the bottom (copy it into a spreadsheet if you prefer).

---

## 0. Quick Background (why this works)

- **Reading speed** for average adults: ~200–250 WPM (words per minute) with ~60–70% comprehension.
- **Sub-vocalization** = silently "saying" every word in your head. It caps your reading speed at roughly your *speaking* speed (~150–250 WPM) because your inner voice can't go faster than your mouth could.
- Skilled speed readers don't eliminate sub-vocalization entirely (100% elimination reduces comprehension on complex/technical text) — they **reduce it** and instead rely on:
  1. **Chunking** — reading groups of 2–4 words per fixation instead of one word at a time.
  2. **Reduced regression** — not re-reading words/lines out of habit.
  3. **Pacing** — using a visual guide (finger, pen, cursor) to pull your eyes forward faster than your inner voice can keep up, which naturally suppresses sub-vocalization.
  4. **Peripheral vision use** — taking in more of the line per fixation.
- Realistic target: 400–700 WPM with 80%+ comprehension for general non-technical material after 6–8 weeks of consistent practice. Technical/dense material will always be slower — that's expected, not a failure.

## 1. Baseline Test (Day 1 — do this before anything else)

1. Pick a ~1,000-word passage of *unfamiliar* non-fiction (not something you've read before). Good sources:
   - [Readsy.org Speed Reading Test](https://readsy.org/speed-reading-test) — built-in WPM + comprehension quiz
   - [Free-Reading-Test.com](https://www.free-reading-test.com/)
   - [Readingsoft.com Speed Reading Test](https://www.readingsoft.com/)
   - [Staples EasyTech Reading Test](https://easyreadertest.com/) (also has an eye-tracking-style visualization of your regressions)
2. Read at your normal pace. Time yourself (stopwatch or the site's built-in timer).
3. Immediately after, answer the comprehension quiz (most of the sites above include one) or write a 5-sentence summary from memory and self-grade against the source.
4. Record: **WPM**, **Comprehension %**, date, and note whether you noticed your lips/tongue/throat moving or an inner "voice" reading along.

This is your baseline — every technique below is measured against it.

## 2. The 6-Week Program

### Week 1 — Awareness & Pacing (goal: interrupt the autopilot)

- [ ] **Day 1**: Baseline test (above).
- [ ] **Day 2–3**: **Pacer drill.** Use your finger, a pen, or the mouse cursor to underline each line as you read, moving *slightly faster* than feels comfortable. The hand leads the eyes; the eyes lead (and outrun) the inner voice. 15 min/day on any article (news, blog, etc.).
- [ ] **Day 4–5**: **Humming/counting drill** (breaks sub-vocalization directly). While reading, hum a constant tone or silently count "1-2-3-1-2-3" on loop. This occupies the vocal/auditory loop so it *can't* narrate the words. Read for 10 min. Comprehension will drop initially — that's expected and temporary.
- [ ] **Day 6**: **RSVP tool** (Rapid Serial Visual Presentation — flashes one word/chunk at a time so there's no time to sub-vocalize). Try:
  - [Spreeder.com](https://www.spreeder.com/app.php) — free, adjustable WPM, paste your own text
  - [ZapReader](https://www.zapreader.com/reader/)
  - Start at 300 WPM, 5 min sessions.
- [ ] **Day 7**: Re-test WPM + comprehension on a fresh unfamiliar passage. Log it.

### Week 2 — Chunking (goal: read groups of words, not single words)

- [ ] **Day 8–10**: **Chunk drill.** Take any paragraph and mentally slice it into 2–3 word chunks (e.g., "The quick brown / fox jumped over / the lazy dog"). Practice fixating once per chunk instead of once per word. Use printed text and literally draw slashes if it helps at first.
- [ ] **Day 11–12**: Continue with RSVP tool but set it to display **2–3 words at a time** instead of 1 (Spreeder supports this — "words per group" setting). This forces chunk-reading.
- [ ] **Day 13**: **Peripheral widening drill.** Pick a line of text and try to see the first and last word of the line without moving your eyes past the 2nd and 2nd-to-last word — i.e., use peripheral vision for the edges. 10 min.
- [ ] **Day 14**: Re-test + log.

### Week 3 — Eliminating Regression (goal: stop involuntary re-reading)

- [ ] **Day 15–17**: **No-regression drill.** Cover text you've already read with an index card or your hand as you move down the page — physically prevents backtracking. 15–20 min/day.
- [ ] **Day 18–19**: Combine pacer + chunking + no-regression on real reading material (an article you actually care about, not throwaway text).
- [ ] **Day 20**: Try a **metronome-paced** session — set a metronome or use [Spreeder](https://www.spreeder.com/app.php) at a fixed WPM ~20% above your last test result and force yourself to keep up, accepting lower comprehension temporarily.
- [ ] **Day 21**: Re-test + log.

### Week 4 — Speed + Comprehension Balance (goal: push speed without collapsing comprehension)

- [ ] **Day 22–24**: Alternate days between "speed mode" (RSVP or pacer at max comfortable speed, comprehension secondary) and "comprehension mode" (normal-ish speed, but using chunking + no regression, actively summarizing after each section).
- [ ] **Day 25–26**: Practice **previewing**: skim headings/topic sentences/bold text for 30–60 seconds before reading a piece in full. This primes your brain with structure, which lets chunking/pacing work better because you're not decoding cold.
- [ ] **Day 27**: Try a long-form article (2,000+ words) end-to-end with all techniques combined.
- [ ] **Day 28**: Re-test + log.

### Week 5 — Material Variety (goal: generalize the skill beyond test passages)

- [ ] Apply techniques to 3 different material types this week, one per session type:
  - [ ] Fiction/narrative (novel chapter)
  - [ ] Technical/work material (docs, reports — expect slower speed here, that's normal)
  - [ ] News/blog articles
- [ ] Note in your log which material type gives the biggest WPM gain and which resists speed gains (usually technical material — don't force high WPM there; comprehension matters more).
- [ ] **Day 35**: Re-test + log.

### Week 6 — Consolidation & New Baseline

- [ ] Daily 15–20 min practice, techniques of your choice based on what worked best for you in weeks 1–5.
- [ ] **Day 42**: Full re-test using the *same baseline test format* from Day 1 (ideally same source site for a fair comparison), plus a fresh comprehension-focused test on a technical passage to make sure comprehension held up, not just speed.
- [ ] Compare to Day 1 baseline. Decide whether to keep pushing speed or shift focus to comprehension/retention (e.g., Cornell notes, active recall) next.

---

## 3. Daily Micro-Habits (stack these onto any reading you already do)

- [ ] Use a pacer (finger/cursor) on **everything** you read for the next 6 weeks, including email and Slack — habit transfers faster with volume.
- [ ] Before reading anything longer than a page, spend 15 seconds previewing headings/structure.
- [ ] Once a week, deliberately read something 20% faster than comfortable and accept you'll miss some detail — this recalibrates your sense of "possible speed."

**Success criteria for this program**: WPM increase of 50–100%+ over baseline with comprehension staying at or above baseline (not just faster — actually retaining the same or more). If comprehension drops more than ~10 points from baseline at your new speed, dial speed back 10–15% and consolidate before pushing further.
